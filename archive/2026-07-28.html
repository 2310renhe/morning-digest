MM-ARC: Multimodal Adaptive Routing of Capital with Robustness-Audited Strategy Pools (https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05080)

  • The research question is how to convert multimodal market history into executable positions while limiting overfitting from repeated strategy search.
  • The method introduced is MM-ARC, which uses aligned chart, numerical, and technical-text views to route capital across trend, reversal, breakout, and exposure-control experts.
  • Key findings include an equal-market Sharpe ratio of 1.33 and maximum drawdown of -13.7 for MM-ARC, compared to 0.53 and -18.3 for the LLMoE-style routing baseline.
  • Named companies or technologies: None

The Fundamental Structure of Risk: From Characteristics to Covariance (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24410)

  • The paper proposes the Characteristic-Driven Dynamic Factor Model (CD-DFM), a non-linear latent factor model that estimates covariance structure from observable firm characteristics.
  • The model is trained on S&P 500 equities and produces economically structured latent representations, interpretable factor portfolios, and competitive covariance forecasts.
  • The CD-DFM relies on substantially lower-frequency information than return-based approaches and can embed previously unseen assets without retraining.
  • No specific companies are mentioned, but the model is applied to S&P 500 equities.
  • Quantitative figures: the model is trained on S&P 500 equities, but no specific numbers or percentages are provided.

Variational Quantum Conditional Boltzmann Machines for Time-Series Forecasting (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24065)

  • The study evaluates four conditional energy-based forecasting architectures: a classical Gaussian-Bernoulli CRBM, a hybrid quantum-classical QCRBM, a full-register QQRBM, and a lag-feature QFeatureQRBM.
  • The architectures are tested on two data classes: a Gaussian-process dataset (GP) generated with real financial data and the input-driven NARMA-10 nonlinear benchmark.
  • The study finds no systematic evidence of a quantum advantage at the available sample size, with the hybrid QCRBM being statistically indistinguishable from the strongest classical CRBM on both datasets.
  • Quantitative figures: thirteen structured experiments are conducted, and the study finds that at n = 12, only medium-to-large effects are detectable.
  • Supply chain or value chain relationships: none explicitly described.
  • Technology transitions: the study compares classical and quantum architectures for time-series forecasting.
  • Competitive positioning: the classical CRBM is found to be the lowest at three of the four budgets, and no CRBM-vs-QCRBM difference is significant at any budget.
